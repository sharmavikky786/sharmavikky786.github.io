SPC to 3D: Novel View Synthesis from Binary SPC via I2I Translation

Indian Institute of Technology Madras, Chennai, India

IEEE Xplore arXiv 2506.06890 PDF BibTeX

Binary single-photon frames lose most colour and texture, so NeRF and Gaussian Splatting reconstruct them poorly. Translating each frame to colour with Pix2PixHD first, then reconstructing, gives the sharpest and most faithful novel views.

The task

A single-photon camera records, for every pixel and colour channel, only whether at least one photon arrived. From a handful of these binary views we want a 3D scene that can be rendered from any new angle, in colour. Drag either image to move the camera.

A toy ray tracer illustrates the problem. These images are not output from the paper's models. The binary view keeps one bit per channel with detection probability 1 − e−λ, so each pixel can only be one of eight colours.

Abstract

Single-photon cameras capture images at very high speed and in very low light, which makes them attractive for 3D reconstruction in conditions where ordinary cameras fail. Their output, however, is binary. Each frame loses most of its texture and colour information, and standard novel view synthesis methods such as NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruct such frames poorly.

We propose a modular two-stage pipeline. An image-to-image translation network first turns each binary SPC frame into a plausible colour image. NeRF or Gaussian Splatting then reconstructs the scene from these images and renders new views. Pix2PixHD works best as the translation stage, helped by its feature-matching adversarial loss. Pipelines built on it beat the other combinations and the single-stage baselines in perceptual quality and geometric consistency.

Pix2PixHD trains with an adversarial loss and a feature-matching loss over several discriminator scales. Findings

  • Pix2PixHD with NeRF gives the highest PSNR (22.70 dB) and SSIM (0.684). Pix2PixHD with Gaussian Splatting gives the lowest LPIPS (0.425).
  • Training NeRF or Gaussian Splatting directly on binary frames gives washed-out colour (NeRF) or visible noise (Gaussian Splatting).
  • Denoising with Restormer or DiffBIR before reconstruction leaves colours too light and texture weak.
  • The pipeline is modular. Each stage can be swapped for a better network as one becomes available.

Next steps are an end-to-end model that optimises translation and reconstruction together, and tests on frames from real SPC sensors.
