Transforming Single Photon Camera Images to Color High Dynamic Range Images

Sumit Sharma, Girish Rongali, Kaushik Mitra

Indian Institute of Technology Madras, Chennai, India

Springer chapter arXiv 2412.12942 PDF BibTeX

A single-photon camera captures very dark and very bright scenes without ghosting, but its images are noisy and monochrome. We test which networks turn them into colour HDR images. Pix2PixHD is best for colour and DeepHDR is best for HDR.

Why SPADs see HDR

A conventional pixel collects charge until its well is full, then clips. A SPAD counts photons one at a time, and after each detection it is blind for a short dead time. As light gets brighter, the count rate approaches one per dead time but never hits a hard ceiling. This soft saturation gives SPADs their wide dynamic range.

Illustrative model of ideal sensors with quantum efficiency 0.45. The conventional pixel has a 10,000 e⁻ full well. The SPAD follows the passive-mode mean count used in the paper. Dynamic range runs from one expected detection per exposure to clipping (conventional) or 95% of the maximum count rate (SPAD). Read noise and dark counts are ignored.

Abstract

CMOS sensors have limited dynamic range. Dark regions drown in electronic noise and bright regions saturate. Fusing several exposures extends the range, but moving scenes then show ghosting and flicker. Single-photon avalanche diode (SPAD) sensors, also called single-photon cameras (SPCs), detect individual photons with precise timing and have no analog-to-digital readout step. Their nonlinear response handles both very low and very high light. The images they produce, however, are noisy, monochrome and hard to look at.

This paper asks which existing networks best turn monochrome SPC images into colour HDR images. We split the problem in two: an image-to-image translation network adds colour, then a single-image HDR network expands the dynamic range. We compare every combination at two resolutions, from one SPC frame and from four averaged frames. We also train a single network that maps SPC frames straight to HDR.

Findings

  • Pix2PixHD is the strongest colourisation network at both resolutions. Its multi-scale discriminators keep colour and texture where Pix2Pix and CycleGAN lose them.
  • Pix2PixHD followed by DeepHDR is the best of the twelve pipelines, with Q = 4.01 from four frames at 2048 × 1024.
  • CycleGAN struggles to keep colours and reconstructs light sources poorly at the higher resolution.
  • Higher resolution and averaging four frames both improve the HDR score for every pipeline.
  • Single-image HDR networks are built to expand the bit depth of LDR photos. Going directly from monochrome SPC to colour HDR needs extra losses, and adversarial losses look promising.

SPADs can record more than 96,000 frames per second. A natural next step is high-frame-rate colour HDR video.
